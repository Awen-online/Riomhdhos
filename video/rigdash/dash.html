Destinations

Ingest from OBS—

Each destination is its own ffmpeg reading the same local RTMP path, so one platform dropping cannot take the others down, and a dead one retries on its own with backoff. Restart relay bounces the whole scheduled task — it does not touch OBS, and OBS keeps sending while it comes back.

Keys live in the relay's own keys.env, never here and never in a log.

Phones

—
Power mode—
⚠️ Both phones DRAIN while streaming and neither port gives more than 500 mA. Sleep stops RigCam, turns High Quality off, releases the screens and puts them to sleep. Normal brings it all back — but leaves High Quality off, because it costs ~170 mA on the phone that runs out first.

Sleep takes the cameras down. That is the point, and the checks on Rig will say so.
Camera bridges—
—
⚠️ feeding means a frame count in the log, not a fresh log. A bridge whose phone is off writes no picture yet … retrying in 5s every five seconds — the freshest log on the machine — and that used to be read as healthy. It now reads no signal. A bridge that says running can still be feeding nothing: when the sink goes away underneath it — OBS closed, the machine slept — it blocks with the process alive and the counter frozen, which is stalled.

Reset this bridge restarts one camera and leaves the other one live; that is the one to reach for mid-set. Force reset both is the sledgehammer, and the only path that also catches an ffmpeg orphaned by a bridge that already died. Either way the bridge re-opens its OBS source once frames are actually flowing, so give it about ten seconds before the picture comes back.

Scene presets

On scene change—
—
⚠️ OBS filters belong to the source, not the scene, so a filter toggled in one scene is toggled in all of them — and zoom is not an OBS property at all, it is a setting on the phone. Neither can be made scene-dependent by OBS alone, so the dashboard watches the program scene and applies these instead.

Set a look up by hand in OBS and on the phones, then press Capture on that scene. Apply runs it now without changing scene.

Zoom takes a second or two to reach the phone, so presets suit a deliberate scene change rather than a fast cut. A scene with no preset is never touched, and nothing is applied when the dashboard starts — only on an actual change.

Cameras

Both phones run RigCam now. The Pixel 8 used to come in over UVC, which offered zoom presets and a quality toggle and nothing else — no exposure, no white balance, which is exactly why the two could never be matched. Give them the same ISO, shutter and warmth and they match by construction instead of drifting apart over a set.

Filters

Configure filters on—
Each camera carries its own filter chain. Grade them to match before cutting between them — two feeds where only one is corrected is exactly what makes a cut jump.

⚠️ Parameters and on/off ONLY. Adding, removing or changing a filter's model reinitialises it while the render thread may be inside it — an access violation in obs_source_skip_video_filter, observed. Do those from OBS itself, before a show.

Measured budget: focal blur ~7 ms, AI low light ~10 ms of a 33.3 ms frame. Colour correction is a GPU shader at ~0.12 ms — if a gamma lift is enough for the room, turning the AI low light off buys back nearly a third of the frame.

Fetched on demand — a wired-camera query runs a UI dump and costs ~2 s, so it is deliberately not in the 1 Hz poll.
